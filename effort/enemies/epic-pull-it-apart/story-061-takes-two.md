# It takes two: one holds it down, one pulls

## Summary

> "making a way of team work for one person to hold it down while some
> one pulls it limbs off"

**One player cannot pull a limb off a giant spider.** One player
holding it still, and a second pulling, can.

## This is the point of the whole epic

Delve already has one cooperative moment, and it works because of a
rule: **struggling alone is never enough** (STO-ENEMIES-035). You
cannot get yourself off a spike; someone has to come for you.

This is the same rule pointed the other way. You cannot take a spider
apart on your own; someone has to help you. Two players, two jobs, and
neither job is the whole thing.

Making both of delve's team moments run on one rule is deliberate — it
is how the game says what it is about.

## How the two jobs differ

They must not be the same job done twice, or it is just "two people
pulling", which is arithmetic rather than teamwork.

- **The holder** keeps the creature — or the limb — still. Their
  problem is staying attached while it thrashes.
- **The puller** applies the force that beats the breaking strain.
  Their problem is that they cannot do it while it is moving.

So the holder is not helping pull. The holder is making pulling
*possible*.

## Definition of Done

- [ ] One player pulling alone cannot remove a limb, however long they
      try. **Tested as a negative** — without this, a limb that comes
      off for anybody would pass every other check here.
- [ ] One player holding plus one pulling can.
- [ ] The two jobs are different: holding is not a second pull.
- [ ] Letting go while the other pulls stops it working.
- [ ] The practice dummy can stand in for one of them, so it can be
      tried by one person (STO-ENEMIES-029).
- [ ] Proven by a headless test running BOTH cases — alone, and
      together — and getting different answers.

## Out of scope

- More than two players making it faster still.
- Holding down the whole spider so it cannot move at all. It is held
  enough to work on, not paralysed.
